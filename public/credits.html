Cathy's Memory Arcade // source room

Credits & context

This is a living memorial by Chad Thompson-Smith for his mother, Cathy. The memories are named as memories, the fiction is named as fiction, and factual claims lead back to their sources.

01 // The five dollars

Arcade history

  • Artie Romero's Colorado Springs video-game history records the 1986 Nickels & Dimes Saturday parties at 710 E. Fillmore, the 1987 Boardwalk opening, and the eventual $5 all-you-can-play admission. Its copyrighted photographs remain linked at their source rather than copied here.
  • The U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics CPI-U table supplies the 1987 annual and June 2026 indices used for the optional buying-power comparison.

02 // What is real

Family material

The photo-booth portraits and celebration-of-life program are family-owned materials supplied and authorized by Chad. The web copies preserve the real photograph; no face or likeness was generated, replaced, or synthesized.

Biographical details about Catherine Denise Smith-Fitts come from the family-authorized program. The public page summarizes her life without publishing its full list of living relatives.

Memorial photographs and family materials remain the family's property. They are not covered by the source-code license.

03 // What is imagined

Games & stories

The arcade environment, game backplates, and story illustrations are AI-assisted original artwork. They do not reproduce commercial characters, cabinet art, or family likenesses.

Skyline Smash, Token Trail, Dungeon Circuit, Highrise Havoc, Sunset Run, and Dragonfire Descent are original browser games made for this memorial. Their names, levels, characters, mechanics, and code-native art use no third-party game assets.

The Last Token, Neon Runner 1986, and The Cabinet That Remembers are original fiction. Their characters and events are not claims about Cathy or the historical Fillmore arcade.

Commercial game titles appear only where Chad remembers playing them. Those names belong to their respective owners.

04 // Inside the jukebox

Sound & privacy

The music and game effects are synthesized in the browser with Web Audio. Fillmore After Dark, Moxie's Midnight Run, Garden Static, Open Road '86, and Free Play Forever are original procedural compositions. Mountain King '86 rearranges Edvard Grieg's public-domain In the Hall of the Mountain King. No voice recording, stream, or borrowed game sound is used.

Your high scores and story progress stay in your browser. The site has no analytics, ads, cookies, accounts, or tracking pixels.

05 // After closing

Signal booth

The automated reel chooses from a small, hand-picked source pool and publishes only a headline, source, date, and direct link. Its play-and-preservation lane includes the nonprofit Video Game History Foundation and The Strong National Museum of Play.
